THE PROBLEM

What if a previous financial crisis happened again?

Financial prediction often focuses on what might happen next. We wanted to explore a different question: what could happen if a historical economic stress scenario occurred again?

We built a platform that uses historical financial and economic data to generate stress scenarios inspired by events such as the 2008 financial crisis and the COVID-19 period, and then examines how companies could respond under those conditions.

The goal was to provide a way to explore potential vulnerabilities under severe economic conditions rather than only looking at ordinary short-term market behaviour.

THE APPROACH

Why we chose financial stress testing.

Instead of trying to predict the next day's market movement, we wanted to explore how companies might respond when conditions resemble a previous period of severe economic stress.

The idea was to use patterns from historical situations to create hypothetical stress scenarios and examine how different financial indicators and companies could respond.

This shifted the focus from ordinary short-term prediction toward understanding potential vulnerabilities under unusual economic conditions.

THE MLOPS LAYER

Why a trained model is only the beginning.

A model that works locally still needs testing, reproducible training, validation, deployment, monitoring, and a way to handle future changes. This was the part of the project that introduced me to the engineering work around an ML model.

A model trained on one developer's machine may work there, but changing data, dependencies, model versions, and deployment environments make it difficult to maintain a consistent system by manually sharing trained models.

That problem becomes even larger when moving from a six-person development team to a system intended to serve many users.

CI/CD helped us think about the ML system as an automated workflow rather than a collection of individual notebooks or locally trained models.

CHALLENGES

The problems we had to solve along the way.

The original design did not work perfectly from the start. Data-fetching issues, model integration, and deployment problems forced us to troubleshoot and change parts of our approach.

01

Financial data collection

We encountered runtime and data-fetching issues as the volume of financial data increased. Repeated problems with the original data source eventually required us to reconsider our approach.

02

Connecting different models

Each model operated on different data and performed a different task. Getting the components to work together around the same end goal was more complicated than working with each model individually.

03

Deployment

Some components that worked during development encountered problems during deployment. This required additional troubleshooting across the application, dependencies, model components, and cloud environment.

04

Changing the data source

When financial data-fetching issues continued, we changed our approach and incorporated the Alpha Vantage API rather than continuing with the original approach alone.

TAKEAWAYS

What this project changed about how I think about ML.

I came into the project thinking primarily about models. I left with a much clearer understanding of everything required to turn those models into a reproducible and maintainable ML system.

I learned how much exists between a trained model and a usable ML product: data collection, validation, integration, testing, deployment, monitoring, versioning, and retraining.

I also learned that MLOps is not simply a collection of tools. It is about creating an automated workflow that makes an ML system reproducible and maintainable as the data, code, models, and environment change.

Working in a six-member team also taught me how to handle conflicting ideas, divide responsibilities, complete work that another person could build on, and keep moving under pressure.

What I would explore further.
